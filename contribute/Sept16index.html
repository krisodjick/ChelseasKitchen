Restoring Algonquin Presence

From acknowledgement to presence.

Ottawa acknowledges every day that it sits on the unceded and unsurrendered territory of the Algonquin Anishinaabe Nation. We want to turn some of that acknowledgement into something people can actually see.

Visible Algonquin food. Visible Algonquin businesses. Visible Algonquin entrepreneurs.

Contribute Read the full project
Why this matters

Acknowledgement and presence are not the same thing.

Walk through the ByWard Market, Sparks Street, Lansdowne or Ottawa's major tourism spaces and there are remarkably few permanent places where Algonquin people are visibly working, selling, creating and participating in the economy of our own traditional territory.

Restoring Algonquin Presence is about changing that in a practical way. Chelsea's Kitchen is working toward mobile commercial carts that can create visible, lower-barrier places for Algonquin entrepreneurs to begin.

The idea

Build the opportunity first.

Four carts. Four highly visible Ottawa spaces. One stronger Algonquin economic presence.
01

Build the carts.

Professionally built mobile vending infrastructure designed to operate in high-visibility public spaces.

02

Prove the model.

Chelsea's Kitchen can help establish the locations, generate traffic and show that the model works.

03

Open the opportunity.

Create a pathway for other Kitigan Zibi entrepreneurs to test ideas without first needing a storefront of their own.

TRC Week 2026

Do something useful now while we build something bigger.

During Truth & Reconciliation Week, Chelsea's Kitchen will also use this campaign to put food directly into people's hands in downtown Ottawa.

For every $5 contributed through this campaign, Chelsea's Kitchen commits to distributing one portion of baked bannock and chili downtown in early October.

That is the immediate commitment. The larger purpose is to keep developing the infrastructure, relationships and operating model behind Restoring Algonquin Presence.
