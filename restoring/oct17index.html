Restoring Algonquin Presence

Build the space. Remove barriers. Create access to the market. Restoring Algonquin Presence is a practical plan to help Algonquin entrepreneurs test, sell and grow in Ottawa through shared infrastructure, strong locations, sales support and partnerships.

Why this exists

Acknowledgement and presence are not the same.

Ottawa's tourism economy is large and concentrated around nationally recognized places. Yet visitors still have few dependable opportunities to find Algonquin-owned businesses, buy directly from Algonquin people, or encounter contemporary Algonquin enterprise as part of the capital experience.

Build a distinct, authentic visitor experience rooted in the people and territory of the capital.

◆

Create affordable access to high-visibility locations for Algonquin entrepreneurs, makers and cultural practitioners.

Economic impact

Generate direct sales, paid work, customer relationships and evidence that helps businesses grow.

Stronger Ottawa

Move beyond symbolic recognition toward a visitor economy that visibly includes Algonquin people.

The goal is not to manufacture demand. It is to place Algonquin businesses inside an existing flow of visitors and spending—and make that presence dependable enough to be found, recommended and returned to.
What it is

Infrastructure for Algonquin entrepreneurs.

Restoring Algonquin Presence is not one business expanding into more carts. It is a shared platform designed to make it easier for Algonquin entrepreneurs to reach real customers in Ottawa. The project brings together locations, modular selling infrastructure, promotion, payment tools, practical training, sales coaching and institutional relationships.

Access

High-visibility Ottawa locations and opportunities that can be difficult or expensive for an individual entrepreneur to access alone.

Infrastructure

Adaptable carts and tables, payment systems, shared promotion and practical operating support so participants can focus on their business.

Sales support

Mandatory practical preparation focused on pricing, presentation, customer engagement, active selling, inventory and learning from real market results.

What it is not.

It is not Chelsea's Kitchen owning a collection of Algonquin businesses. Participating entrepreneurs keep their own identity, products, pricing and customer relationships.

It is not about speaking for Algonquin people or requiring anyone to perform culture in order to participate.

And it is not another training program that prepares people and then leaves them to solve the hardest problem alone: finding a market. The market access is part of the model.

Universal cart system

Infrastructure that can change with the people using it.

The physical setup is being designed as a flexible platform rather than a one-size-fits-all storefront. Modular carts, tables and displays can change with the entrepreneur using them, while a common Restoring Algonquin Presence identity helps customers recognize the network.

Recognizable

One Restoring Algonquin Presence identity makes the network easy for visitors, hotels and tourism partners to find and recommend.

Independent

Participating entrepreneurs keep control of their businesses, products, pricing, customer relationships and intellectual property.

For entrepreneurs

Start with the person and the business idea.

The pilot is being built for Algonquin people with existing businesses, products, skills or early-stage ideas who could benefit from access to a larger Ottawa market. The goal is to lower the cost and complexity of testing an idea while still expecting participants to operate professionally and actively sell.

Express interest

Tell us what you make, sell or want to test, what stage you are at and what support would help.

Prepare

Work through pricing, product presentation, compliance, inventory, customer engagement and practical sales training before operating.

Get access

Use shared infrastructure and an appropriate Ottawa sales opportunity without having to build a downtown storefront first.

Sell and learn

Meet real customers, gather evidence, adjust the offer and build the confidence and information needed for the next step.

The visitor experience

Easy to recognize. Easy to find. Worth returning to.

Restoring Algonquin Presence is not a souvenir stop or a symbolic reconciliation installation. It is a living marketplace: Algonquin people operating businesses, selling work, sharing approved experiences and participating directly in Ottawa's visitor economy.

Arrive

A clear Algonquin-led marketplace identity explains what is operating that day.

Explore

A reliable food anchor alongside changing products, demonstrations and guest concepts.

Purchase

Straightforward transactions that create direct economic benefit for participating Algonquin businesses.

Return

A changing residency calendar gives visitors and Ottawa residents a reason to come back.

Project principles

Algonquin-led. Commercially grounded. Culturally responsible.

Presence, not symbolism

Success means visible space, paid work, sales and repeat participation—not acknowledgement alone.

Direct economic benefit

Activity must create measurable value for participating Algonquin businesses and contributors.

Business independence

No entrepreneur is required to perform culture in order to sell a product. Cultural participation is voluntary and appropriately compensated.

Accountability

Selection, fees, sponsorships, conflicts, consent and results are documented and transparent.

Building it step by step

This is a build, not a launch announcement.

The project began by testing the central idea: what can happen when an Algonquin business gets direct access to Ottawa customers? From there, each stage is meant to answer the next practical question before the pilot opens.

September 2026

Prove the idea

Use TRC Week workplace lunches to build relationships, enter new Ottawa workplaces and test the larger idea of market access. Begin formal business-plan development for Restoring Algonquin Presence.

October 2026

Build the foundation

Launch the Growing Prize Raffle for initial operating resources, continue business-plan development, begin grant applications, develop the advisory structure and advance conversations with potential partners and locations.

November 2026

Recruit & listen

Begin outreach across Algonquin communities to people with businesses, products, skills or ideas. Start conversations with economic-development offices and learn directly from prospective participants about the barriers they face.

December 2026

Shape the pilot

Review interest, hold individual conversations and refine the model around real needs such as transportation, equipment, inventory, permits, payments and selling support. Continue funding and site development.

January 2027

Build the program

Identify an initial pilot group and finalize training, operating standards, shared infrastructure, payment systems, branding expectations and the support entrepreneurs receive.

February 2027

Secure the market

Move promising location and institutional conversations toward commitments. Match potential participants with the kinds of Ottawa opportunities that make sense for their products.

March 2027

Prepare to sell

Begin practical entrepreneur preparation around pricing, presentation, customer engagement, active selling, inventory and operating logistics. Advance permits, insurance and site requirements.

April 2027

Build & test

Prepare carts, tables, signage and payment systems. Run smaller tests where possible, solve operating problems and make sure entrepreneurs and infrastructure are ready before the public launch.

May 2027

Launch the pilot

Begin bringing participating Algonquin entrepreneurs into Ottawa sales locations. Promote the network while keeping each entrepreneur's own business visible and independent.

Summer 2027

Sell, learn & grow

Operate, coach, test locations and build relationships with workplaces, events, tourism partners and institutions. Adjust the model using what entrepreneurs and customers actually show us.

Fall 2027

Evaluate & expand

Document what worked, what did not and what needs to change. Develop colder-season opportunities and use the evidence to decide how the next version should grow.

Partnerships

Help create the conditions for real presence.

The project needs more than a cart. It needs strong locations, affordable access, tourism distribution, entrepreneur referrals, technical expertise and partners willing to invest in measurable outcomes.

Site partners

Offer a recurring, high-visibility location with workable permissions, utilities, storage, accessibility and promotion.

Tourism partners

Help visitors discover the network through hotels, attractions, conferences, tour itineraries, events and destination marketing.

Sponsors & funders

Support cart capital, sponsored residencies, paid Algonquin guidance, programming, accessibility, marketing and evaluation.
